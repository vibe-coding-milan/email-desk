## 3. Shared organization principles

P01 — Use one vocabulary across both accounts. Account identity is metadata, not a topic. A label may be quiet or empty in one account without needing removal or an account-specific replacement. New account-specific labels should require a demonstrated need.

P02 — Classify by the actual purpose of the message. The same sender may generate purchases, refunds, security alerts, advertising, and direct correspondence. PayPal does not always mean Financial; eBay work correspondence does not mean Shopping; a Google Drive share does not automatically mean Accounts & Services.

P03 — Use a main topic and an additional label when both improve retrieval. A secondary label is complementary, not an alternative answer. The primary/secondary distinction exists in review records; Gmail itself does not encode label order or a primary-label field. Two labels are a useful default, not a hard Gmail limit.

P04 — Separate topic, record type, and handling. Dining Out describes a purchase context; Records/Receipts & Statements describes its documentary purpose. Needs Review describes unresolved classification. Inbox visibility, urgency, reading cadence, and whether action is required are separate decisions.

P05 — Keep important correspondence visible. Milan accepts aggressive organization but does not want consequential messages to slip past him. Topic labeling alone should not remove messages from the inbox, mark them read, or imply they are handled. Start any future filing automation with narrow rules and explicit exceptions.

P06 — Keep Vetro condo governance separate from Manhattan Community Board 5. Home covers private housing matters, including renovation. Housing-related context can overlap, but a noisy-neighbor matter for Milan’s residence need not be condo-board business.

P07 — Preserve Mom deliberately. Her correspondence has lasting value independent of volume or age. Do not place it in miscellaneous archive, treat it as disposable because it is old, or infer someone is Milan’s mother from a name alone.

P08 — Avoid vague catch-all categories such as Life Administration and Personal Archive. Use a specific existing topic, a record label where it genuinely fits, or Needs Review with an explicit question. Do not force a false topic just to eliminate unlabeled messages.

P09 — Apply nested labels intentionally. A Gmail child label does not automatically apply its parent to the message. Do not add the parent merely to inflate its count, and do not interpret an empty parent as an unused family.

P10 — Prefer durable subject relationships over broad platform rules. Recognize Nina and her salon across business-name and booking-platform changes. Do not classify all Setmore, Vagaro, Google Calendar, Google Drive, or Square messages identically.

## 4. Implemented label dictionary

Misdirected — Mail verified as intended for somebody else. Remove false personal-topic labels; do not infer Milan’s interests or circumstances from these messages. This does not itself report spam, archive mail, or block a sender.

Accounts & Services/Authentication — Routine one-time codes, magic links, and email or device verification. Excludes substantive security alerts, changed access, and inactivity/deletion warnings. Labeling does not imply inbox bypass or deletion.

## 6. Inbox handling and exception treatment

Current default: add topic labels without changing Inbox or read state. The initial migration did not create ongoing filing rules. Milan prefers consequential mail to stay visible and is open to aggressive filing where importance is protected.

Proposed future automation for routine authentication: label narrowly first, verify representative matches in both accounts, and only then consider inbox bypass. Do not automatically delete codes or apply a sender-wide rule that also hides security alerts. No timed deletion or retention schedule is approved.

Adopted classification of confirmed misdirection: segregate it from Milan’s personal topics. Any archive, deletion, sender block, unsubscribe, or contact correction is a separate action. Avoid blanket blocking organizations or shared platforms that also send Milan relevant mail. Do not access or alter someone else’s account to correct an address.

Spam handling: use Gmail’s system Spam / phishing controls for messages Milan identifies as spam or suspicious. Do not create a custom Spam label as a substitute. Milan identified the ELLE / Welcome Media newsletters and Triglav survey as spam, and the crextu.com calendar-style notice as potentially phishing. Reporting has not yet been performed. False custom labels were removed from the reviewed ELLE / Welcome Media and Triglav messages without reporting or moving them. The suspicious crextu.com invitation retains Needs Review. Confirm exact matches before any separate reporting action.

Formerly relevant subscriptions: distinguish unsubscribe from spam reporting. Fourth Street East was a genuine Oakland housing search with Milan’s fiancée, but is no longer relevant after leaving the West Coast. It is an unsubscribe candidate. Care.com is a paid membership Milan meant to end; cancelling it requires a separate subscription action, not merely unsubscribing from email. Neither action has been executed.

Needs Review handling: use the label for a precise unresolved question, such as “Which person is this?”, “Is this the Vetro building?”, or “Does this support an active project?” Do not use it solely because a subject contains “urgent,” “verify,” or “action required.” After classification is resolved, removal of Needs Review should accompany the authorized correction; unresolved action should not be hidden or assumed complete. A dedicated action workflow remains a future decision.

Correspondence preservation: keep mother-related material and meaningful personal exchanges. No age-based disposal rule is approved. Authentication, misdirected mail, spam, and receipts also have no newly approved timed deletion policy. This section governs handling distinctions, not a blanket retention schedule for message deletion.

## 7. Responsibility and change control

Milan owns the final taxonomy, personal context, exceptions, and decisions about what deserves attention. The assistant can analyze messages, propose rules, and execute changes within Milan’s current authorized scope. Gmail filters apply only the exact implemented conditions; no standing assistant classification service exists unless separately configured.

Before future migration work, read the current master playbook and check the relevant Gmail label inventory. Use the exact label names. Do not treat a historical chat recommendation, review-workbook suggestion, or proposed rule as an implemented filter.

For a rule change, record: rule ID; date; reason and representative example; scope and exceptions; decision status; whether Gmail labels, existing message associations, or filters were actually changed; and the evidence used to verify the change. Update the master in place and refresh the Markdown companion. Keep stable rule IDs when wording is clarified.

Use one shared rule across both accounts unless a concrete exception requires otherwise. Preserve account-specific facts without duplicating or diverging the overall taxonomy. Do not automatically synchronize or overwrite one copy from another; resolve the master’s latest version first.
